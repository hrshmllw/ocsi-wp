# OCSI designer brief

**Organization:** Optimized Customer Solutions, Inc. (OCSI)
**Market:** Philippines
**What this is:** A standalone brief for designers. It carries the purpose, the audiences, the tone, the visual system, and the intent of each page.

The project brief, the visual direction, and the feature specs remain the source of truth. This document summarizes them so a designer can start without opening those files. Links are at the end.

**Status:** Direction A is the recommended starting point. Directions B and C stay open for exploration. The palette still needs stakeholder approval. Page copy on the site is a draft from public profiles and is not approved company messaging.

---

## 1. OCSI in one paragraph

Optimized Customer Solutions, Inc. is a Philippine FMCG distribution company. Public profiles date the start to 2002 in Pampanga. OCSI supplies Nestlé grocery products and Nestlé Professional food-service products to retailers, wholesalers, public markets, sari-sari stores, restaurants, offices, and other food-service customers. Coverage named in those profiles runs through Luzon (Pampanga, Cavite, Laguna, Quezon, Marinduque, Las Piñas, Muntinlupa, and Parañaque) and Western Visayas (Iloilo, Antique, and Guimaras from August 2019).

The website is OCSI’s official digital presence. Its job is corporate credibility: who the company is, what it does, where it operates, and how to reach it or apply for work. It is a company site for partners and applicants. It does not sell products.

---

## 2. The feeling

The site should leave five impressions:

- **Established.** A company with years of operation and a real network.
- **Trustworthy.** Clear information, authentic photography, and only claims the company has approved.
- **Capable.** Distribution, sales execution, logistics, and coverage presented with confidence.
- **People-oriented.** The teams who do the work are visible.
- **Contemporary.** Clean type, intentional space, responsive layouts, and restrained motion.

### Aim for

A premium corporate layout with human, FMCG photography. Refined structure, a restrained palette, then warmth from real people, facilities, delivery work, and retail environments. The company should read as a serious distribution partner that still feels current and human.

### Stay away from

- A warehouse-management dashboard or operations software look.
- An online store, product catalog, or consumer-app chrome (heavy rounding, bright gradients, icon-only cards).
- A generic corporate template with a centered headline over a darkened stock photo.
- The visual language of a product manufacturer. OCSI distributes. Nestlé makes the products.
- Invented scale: outlet counts, fleet size, workforce totals, coverage percentages, awards, or endorsements that have not been approved.

## 3. Who visits and what they need

| Visitor | They need | Send them to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prospective business partners and brand principals | Background, credibility, capabilities, and a way to inquire | About, Capabilities, Partners, Contact |
| Existing stakeholders | Official company facts and how to reach a site | About, Network, Contact |
| Job seekers | Culture, open roles, and how to apply | Careers, vacancy pages |
| General public | A reliable account of who OCSI is | Home, About, Network |

Design for corporate credibility before promotional effect. Internal editors update copy, branches, and vacancies after launch. Give them clear content areas. Keep layout decisions in the design system.

## 4. Visual directions

All three directions share one palette. They differ in how much red or blue is emphasized, and in how active the photography and layout feel.

**Start with Direction A.** Bring in selected warmth from Direction B so the site stays human and specific to FMCG distribution. Direction C is the alternative if stakeholders want a stricter operational tone.

### Direction A — Modern, premium corporate (recommended)

A refined corporate style: corporate red, corporate blue, cool neutrals, spacious layouts, large headings, and strong photography.

- Cool off-white page, white navigation, corporate red for the main action, corporate blue for links and a few headings.
- Oversized but readable headlines, generous whitespace, asymmetrical heroes.
- Large authentic photography. Subtle borders. Restrained cards. Minimal animation.
- Photography: warehouses and distribution, delivery vehicles and facilities, field sales, staff and leadership, branch environments.
- Impression: established, capable, trustworthy, contemporary.
- Best when the company wants to be read as a serious distribution partner without looking dated or stiff.

### Direction B — FMCG-focused and energetic

A brighter direction built around people, retail presence, and the pace of distribution.

- The same tokens, with corporate red used on more calls to action.
- Bold geometric sans-serif type, tighter grids, image cards, and more prominent actions.
- Photography: products in stores, sales visits, grocery shelves and merchandising, delivery teams.
- Impression: approachable, dynamic, people-driven.
- Best when recruitment and employee identity sit almost level with business partnerships.

### Direction C — Enterprise logistics

A structured, operational style. Corporate blue and slate carry the structure. Corporate red stays in small marks.

- Strong grid alignment, structured capability sections, network maps, and verified statistics only.
- Minimal decoration. Highly legible type.
- Photography: facilities, vehicles, distribution centers, operational teams.
- Impression: precise, efficient, established, business-oriented.
- Best when the primary audience is brand principals and corporate partners.

The result of A plus selected B should feel premium and still show OCSI’s real operations. A layout that is so minimal that the warehouses, routes, and people disappear has missed the brief.

## 5. Palette

Recommended set. Stakeholder approval is still open. If OCSI supplies brand guidelines, those override this table.

The logo file uses pure red `#FF0000` and pure blue `#0000FF`. Keep that artwork as supplied. The homepage mockup sets the interface colors. Button red stays `#C62828`. Match it to the official logo red if the official red differs from the file.

| Token | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accent | Button red | `#C62828` | Primary actions. Long text stays off red. |
| Accent | Deep red | `#A20513` | Eyebrows, the current navigation item, timeline marks |
| Link | Blue | `#315EA0` | Links, the outlined header button, selected labels |
| Background | Cool off-white | `#F7F8FA` | Page background |
| Surface | White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, navigation, the logo ground |
| Surface | Ice | `#EEF4FF` | Tiles and note panels |
| Text | Ink | `#161C24` | Headings and body |
| Muted | Slate | `#64748B` | Metadata, captions, secondary labels. Slate is too light for long paragraphs. |

White on `#C62828` is about 5.6:1, so red buttons can carry white labels. White on `#315EA0` is about 5.9:1. Ink on cool off-white is the default reading pair.

Rules:

- Place the logo on white so both colors in the mark stay visible.
- Do not add green, gold, or a third accent hue.
- Direction A: red for the main action, blue for links and a few headings.
- Direction B: the same tokens, with red on more actions.
- Direction C: the same tokens, with blue on more structure and red kept small.

Before the palette is locked: confirm the logo file is the official mark, request any existing brand guidelines, retest white-on-red and white-on-blue, and get stakeholder sign-off.

## 6. Logo

The supplied mark is `wp-content/themes/ocsi/assets/images/ocsi-logo.svg`. It is a circular emblem: a red ring, a blue sweeping form, and a red inner shape. Fills stay `#FF0000` and `#0000FF`. Do not recolor the file to the interface palette.

- Always sit the mark on white.
- Use it in the header with the company name.
- Give it clear space. Do not place it on photography, on corporate red, or on corporate blue.
- Do not redraw, outline, or add a tagline inside the mark.

## 7. Typography

- **Headings:** Plus Jakarta Sans.
- **Body:** Inter.
- **Optional accent:** a restrained serif for a few large headlines, if an editorial feel is wanted. Use it rarely.

Principles:

- One small, consistent scale.
- Headings short and descriptive. Avoid thin weights at display sizes.
- Comfortable paragraph line height. Body text readable on a phone.
- Little all-caps. An eyebrow label can be small uppercase, used sparingly.
- Navigation, forms, and buttons stay legible at small sizes.

| Element | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Large, bold, short, specific to OCSI |
| Page title | Clear and descriptive |
| Section heading | Medium to large, obvious hierarchy |
| Body | Neutral, readable, concise |
| Eyebrow | Small uppercase, rare |
| Action | A verb, with a visible hover, focus, and disabled state |

The earlier concept line “Moving Brands Forward.” is not the homepage. The draft headline in the theme is “Nestlé products, distributed by OCSI.” Design around the draft story in section 11 until OCSI approves final lines.

## 8. Photography

Authentic photography is what makes the site credible. The theme currently shows empty photo slots labeled as not yet supplied.

### Subjects

- Employees and leadership, once OCSI approves names and portraits.
- Warehouse and distribution work.
- Delivery vehicles and facilities.
- Sales and field work.
- The six branch environments: San Fernando, Imus, San Pablo City, Gumaca, Las Piñas, and Oton.
- Retail shelves and products in context, only with permission to show those brands.
- People working together.

### Treatment

- Wide, high-resolution photos for heroes. Text on one side of the image, with enough contrast to read.
- One image ratio inside cards and grids.
- Natural light and real locations. Crop around people and activity.
- WebP or AVIF where the build supports them, with descriptive alt text on meaningful images.

### Avoid

- Generic stock meetings, handshakes as the main visual language, or the same photo reused across sections.
- Pictures that imply facilities or services OCSI does not have.
- Unapproved brand logos or product imagery.
- Full-bleed backgrounds that hide the type or slow the page.

If original photography is not ready, use properly licensed stand-ins during design and mark them as temporary. Replace them with approved OCSI assets before launch.

## 9. Components

### Buttons

- Primary: `#C62828` with white text.
- Secondary: `#315EA0` text or border on white.
- Short labels (“Discover OCSI”, “Explore Our Network”, “Contact our team”).
- Hover, focus, disabled, and active states. Touch targets large enough on a phone.

### Cards

- Consistent padding and image ratio.
- A hairline border or a very light shadow.
- Modest corner radius so the site does not read as a consumer app.
- Short copy and a descriptive text link. Do not make the whole card look clickable unless it is.

### Navigation

Header: logo, About OCSI, Capabilities, Our Network, Our Partners, Careers, and a restrained Contact Us action.

- Simple desktop bar. No mega menu unless content volume later requires one.
- Clear current-page and keyboard-focus states.
- An accessible collapsible menu on small screens.

### Forms

Contact will eventually collect: full name, work email, company name, inquiry category, contact number, message, and a privacy acknowledgment. Recruitment stays on Careers.

- Visible labels, not placeholder-only labels.
- Clear errors, a confirmation state, and keyboard focus.
- The form is not active yet. Design the fields and the success and error states. Do not imply the form already sends mail.

### Icons

One family. Icons support text and photography. Do not mix outline and filled styles without a system.

### Motion

Short transitions that confirm a click, a menu, or a focus change. No scroll-jacking, no parallax that delays the content, and a reduced-motion path that leaves the page fully usable.

## 10. Sitemap and page intent

### Header

Logo, About OCSI, Capabilities, Our Network, Our Partners, Careers, Contact Us.

About can hold Company overview, History, and Mission, vision, and values as sections on one page. Capabilities can hold Distribution, Sales and market execution, and Logistics as anchored sections. Separate child pages only if a section grows enough to need its own URL.

Utility screens to design as well: Privacy Policy, a 404 page, form confirmation, and search results if search is in the design.

### Home

**Purpose:** Say what OCSI is, show why it is credible, and point people to company, capabilities, network, careers, or contact.

**Visual emphasis:** Large operations photograph, short positioning, three capability highlights, a network glimpse, proof only where it is verified.

**Section order:**

1. Header
2. Hero. Draft eyebrow “Since 2002”. Draft headline “Nestlé products, distributed by OCSI.” Supporting line about supplying retailers, wholesalers, sari-sari stores, and food-service customers from Luzon and Western Visayas. Primary action: Discover OCSI. Secondary: Explore Our Network. Photo on one side, not a centered headline on a dark stock image.
3. Company introduction, with a link to About
4. Where the work happens (channels and warehouse locations)
5. Capabilities: three photography-led cards (distribution, sales execution, logistics), each with a heading, a short paragraph, and a text link
6. Distribution network: a few branch highlights and a link to the directory. No map until the territory is confirmed.
7. Partners and recognition: Nestlé as principal. No logos or awards until approved.
8. People and careers, plus any open roles
9. Closing contact action
10. Footer

### About OCSI

**Purpose:** Trust through identity, history, and values.

**Visual emphasis:** Editorial storytelling, a simple timeline, team photography when it exists.

**Content to design for:** company overview, a dated history (2002 Pampanga, 2005 Cavite and Laguna and later Quezon, Marinduque, and southern Metro Manila, 2007 Nestlé Professional, a San Fernando warehouse, 2018 Las Piñas and South Greater Manila food service, August 2019 Iloilo covering Iloilo, Antique, and Guimaras), and an honest empty state for mission, vision, values, and leadership. Sister companies may be mentioned only as unnamed until OCSI names them. End with a link to Capabilities.

### Our Capabilities

**Purpose:** Show operational value to a prospective partner.

**Visual emphasis:** Photography-led sections and a clear service hierarchy. One page with anchors is enough at launch.

**Sections:** overview, distribution operations, sales and market execution, logistics and warehousing, territory coverage, then a partnership contact action. Quality or process sections can be added when OCSI documents them. Do not design stat callouts for outlet counts or fleet size.

### Our Network

**Purpose:** Make the geographic footprint understandable.

**Visual emphasis:** Regional grouping and branch cards. A map is optional and stays out until OCSI confirms the territory.

**Coverage to reflect:** Luzon as listed above, Western Visayas as Iloilo, Antique, and Guimaras. Marinduque is coverage, not a separate office.

**Each branch card and branch detail page:** branch name, address, region or service area, phone and email when OCSI supplies them, hours when confirmed, a directions link, and an optional facility image. Empty phone and hours should look intentional, not broken.

**Draft branches already named:** San Fernando, Pampanga; Imus, Cavite; San Pablo City, Laguna; Gumaca, Quezon; Las Piñas; Oton, Iloilo. Treat the list and the street addresses as unconfirmed drafts.

### Our Partners

**Purpose:** Credibility through verified relationships.

**Visual emphasis:** A clean partnership story. Logo slots and an awards row that can stay empty.

**Rules:** Say “distributor.” Do not design lockups that say “exclusive” or “authorized” until OCSI picks one. Do not place Nestlé or product logos in the layout until written permission exists. Product names that may be set as text, once approved: Nescafé, Milo, Bear Brand, Nestea, Coffee-mate, Nido, Maggi, Chuckie, Nesvita, Nestogen, Nan, KitKat, Crunch, Fox’s, and Polo. Awards need a name, a year, and permission.

### Careers

**Purpose:** Present OCSI as an employer and make open roles easy to find.

**Visual emphasis:** People photography, culture, and accessible job cards.

**Content:** where people work (the six sites), a benefits block that can read “to be confirmed,” how to apply, and a list of open positions that can be empty. Do not invent a sample vacancy or a benefits list. Applications leave the site through an approved link. The design does not include CV upload.

### Vacancy detail

**Purpose:** One job, readable, with a clear Apply action.

**Fields:** job title, location, employment type, department, responsibilities, qualifications, application instructions, and an optional closing date. Apply is a link. Show a clear state when a role is closed.

### Branch detail

**Purpose:** One location, with the facts that are actually known.

**Visual emphasis:** Address first, then region, phone, hours, and a directions link. Missing phone or hours stay blank with a quiet label, not a fake number.

### Contact Us

**Purpose:** A dependable route to the right team.

**Visual emphasis:** A simple form layout, headquarters details, and links to Careers and the branch directory.

**Content:** the San Fernando address as a draft (MacArthur Highway, Barangay Saguin, City of San Fernando, Pampanga). No phone or email until OCSI provides them. Design the form described in section 9, including success and failure. Recruitment questions route to Careers.

## 11. Content reality

Design against this picture. The sentences on the current site are drafts from public profiles (Jobstreet, PhilJobNet, an FDA establishment record, and public job listings). Every page carries a visible note that the copy must be confirmed before publication. Final lines need OCSI approval.

### Safe to design around, still marked draft

- The company distributes Nestlé products, including Nestlé Professional, and has done so in some form since 2002.
- Channels: retail, wholesale, public markets, sari-sari stores, restaurants, offices, and other food service.
- The history dates in the About section above.
- Six branch names and the street addresses that a public listing already printed. Phone and hours are empty on purpose.
- Three capability themes: distribution, field sales, warehousing.

### Leave as an empty or “to be supplied” state

- Mission, vision, values, and leadership names and portraits.
- Official phone numbers and email addresses.
- Partner logos and any word other than “distributor” for the Nestlé relationship.
- Awards and recognition.
- Benefits and any sample job.
- Original photography.
- Outlet counts, fleet size, workforce totals, and coverage percentages.
- A map.

Do not invent statistics, partner logos, or endorsements to fill a layout. If a module needs a number to look finished, design the module so it works without the number, or mark the number as a placeholder that cannot ship.

## 12. Accessibility and performance

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA practices while designing, not as a pass at the end.

- Contrast as in the palette section. Check text on photographs.
- Keyboard access and a visible focus state on links, buttons, and fields.
- A real heading hierarchy. One page title, then sections.
- Labels on every field. Meaningful alt text. Do not put essential text only inside an image.
- Reduced motion that still exposes all content.
- Controls that work with a finger.
- Hero images sized for the slot. No autoplay background video unless a later decision justifies it.
- Test the mobile menu, the contact states, a branch card, and a vacancy card at phone width.

## 14. What we need from the designer

1. A short recommendation on Direction A, with any Direction B elements worth keeping, or a reasoned case for B or C.
2. Color and type tokens, after checking the logo-on-white rule.
3. Sitemap and header/footer.
4. Homepage wireframe, then desktop and mobile homepage.
5. Templates for About, Capabilities, Network, Partners, Careers, and Contact, including empty states from section 11.
6. Branch card, branch detail, vacancy card, and vacancy detail.
7. Button, navigation, card, and form states (hover, focus, error, success, disabled).
8. Image and icon notes, including how temporary photos are marked.
9. Handoff notes a developer can build from: spacing, type scale, and component rules.

### Approval checkpoints

1. Direction, color, type, and photography style.
2. Homepage structure and navigation.
3. Inner pages and reusable components, including empty states.
4. Responsive layouts and interactive states.
5. Final content placed into the design, before build.
