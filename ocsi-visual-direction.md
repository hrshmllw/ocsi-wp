## Visual Direction and Design Inspiration

**Organization:** Optimized Customer Solutions, Inc. (OCSI)  
**Project:** Corporate website redesign/build  
**Platform:** WordPress  
**Document status:** Initial visual direction for exploration, subject to brand and stakeholder approval

---

## 1. Design Objective

Create a polished, modern corporate website that positions OCSI as an established, capable, and trustworthy FMCG distribution partner in the Philippines.

The design should communicate operational strength without feeling like a warehouse management platform, an online store, or a generic corporate template.

### Desired brand impression

- **Established:** A company with experience and reliable operations.
- **Trustworthy:** Clear information, authentic photography, and credible claims.
- **Capable:** Strong presentation of distribution, sales execution, logistics, and network coverage.
- **People-oriented:** Recognize the employees and teams who make the business work.
- **Contemporary:** Clean typography, intentional spacing, responsive layouts, and restrained motion.

### Recommended design direction

Combine a **premium corporate visual style** with **human, FMCG-focused photography**.

Use a refined layout and restrained palette, then add warmth through genuine images of people, facilities, delivery operations, and retail environments.

This is a proposed direction, not a representation of OCSI's current official brand identity. Review existing brand guidelines and logo assets before locking the final design.

## 3. Three Visual Directions to Explore

### Direction A: Modern, Premium Corporate

**Recommended as the primary direction.**

A refined, confident corporate style using deep green, warm white, charcoal, spacious layouts, large headings, and strong photography.

**Visual characteristics**
- Deep forest green and warm neutral backgrounds.
- Oversized but readable headlines.
- Generous whitespace.
- Asymmetrical hero layouts.
- Large, authentic photography.
- Subtle borders and restrained card treatments.
- Minimal animation.

**Photography**
- Warehouse and distribution operations.
- Delivery vehicles and facilities.
- Field sales teams.
- Staff and leadership.
- Branch environments.

**Desired impression:** Established, capable, trustworthy, contemporary.

**Best suited for:** A company that wants to be perceived as a serious distribution partner without appearing dated or overly formal.

### Direction B: FMCG-Focused and Energetic

A brighter, more active direction emphasizing people, retail presence, and the pace of distribution.

**Visual characteristics**
- Bright white base with a bold accent color.
- Bold geometric sans-serif typography.
- Tighter grids and image cards.
- Prominent calls to action.
- More colorful, active photography.
- Clear employee and recruitment sections.

**Photography**
- Products in stores.
- Sales representatives and retail visits.
- Grocery shelves and merchandising.
- Delivery activities and teams.

**Desired impression:** Approachable, dynamic, people-driven.

**Best suited for:** A company where recruitment and employee identity are nearly as important as business partnerships.

### Direction C: Enterprise Logistics

A structured, operationally focused style using navy, slate, white, clean grids, and technical clarity.

**Visual characteristics**
- Navy and slate palette.
- Strong grid alignment.
- Structured capability sections.
- Network maps and verified statistics.
- Minimal decorative elements.
- Highly legible typography.

**Photography**
- Facilities and warehouse operations.
- Vehicles and logistics processes.
- Distribution centers.
- Operational teams.

**Desired impression:** Precise, efficient, established, business-oriented.

**Best suited for:** A company whose primary audience is prospective brand principals and corporate partners.

### Recommended choice

Use **Direction A as the foundation**, with selected elements from Direction B.

The result should feel premium and professional, but still human and relevant to FMCG distribution. Avoid making the site so minimal that it loses OCSI's real-world operational identity.

## 4. Proposed Color Palette

The following palette is a starting point for design exploration, not OCSI's official palette.

| Token | Color | Hex | Suggested use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Forest Green | `#183D32` | Header, hero backgrounds, primary actions |
| Background | Warm Ivory | `#F7F5EF` | Main page background and editorial sections |
| Text | Charcoal | `#252927` | Body text and headings |
| Secondary | Sage | `#DCE7DC` | Soft panels, feature backgrounds, supporting UI |
| Accent | Muted Gold | `#D6A84B` | Small highlights, milestone details, selected accents |
| Surface | White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, forms, and contrast areas |
| Border | Soft Gray | `#DADFD9` | Dividers, card borders, form boundaries |

### Color usage guidelines

- Use forest green as the primary brand-like color only after confirming it works with OCSI's logo and existing identity.
- Use warm ivory and white to create space and avoid large areas of dark color on every page.
- Use charcoal for readable text.
- Use sage sparingly for secondary panels and visual variation.
- Reserve muted gold for small accents. Do not use it for long text or small text on white backgrounds without checking contrast.
- Do not introduce too many accent colors.

### Brand validation

Before finalizing the palette:
1. Obtain OCSI's official logo in vector or high-resolution format.
2. Request any existing brand guidelines.
3. Confirm whether existing corporate colors must be retained.
4. Test text and interface contrast.
5. Get stakeholder approval for the final palette.

## 5. Typography

### Suggested pairing

- **Headings:** Manrope or Plus Jakarta Sans.
- **Body:** Inter.
- **Optional editorial accent:** A restrained serif font for selected large headlines, if a more premium editorial feel is desired.

### Typography principles

- Use a small, consistent type scale.
- Keep headings concise and descriptive.
- Avoid extremely thin weights for large headings.
- Use comfortable line height for paragraphs.
- Keep body text readable on mobile.
- Avoid excessive all-caps text.
- Ensure navigation, forms, and buttons remain legible at smaller sizes.

### Suggested hierarchy

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Hero headline | Large, bold, short, and benefit-oriented |
| Page title | Clear and descriptive |
| Section heading | Medium to large, with strong hierarchy |
| Body copy | Neutral, readable, and concise |
| Eyebrow label | Small uppercase text, used sparingly |
| CTA | Clear action verb and visible interaction state |

## 6. Photography and Image Art Direction

Authentic photography will be one of the most important factors in making the site feel credible.

### Preferred image subjects

- OCSI employees and leadership.
- Warehouse and distribution operations.
- Delivery vehicles and facilities.
- Sales and field operations.
- Branch environments.
- Retail shelves and product distribution in context.
- Employee collaboration and workplace culture.

### Image treatment

- Prefer wide, high-resolution photos for hero sections.
- Use consistent image ratios within cards and grids.
- Favor natural lighting and authentic environments.
- Use real employees and locations where possible.
- Crop images intentionally around people and activity.
- Optimize image sizes and use modern formats such as WebP or AVIF where supported.
- Provide descriptive alternative text for meaningful images.

### Avoid

- Generic stock photos of unrelated office meetings.
- Images that imply facilities or services OCSI does not have.
- Unapproved brand logos or product imagery.
- Overly staged handshakes as the primary visual language.
- Reusing the same stock photo across multiple sections.
- Huge background images that reduce text legibility or performance.

### Content dependency

If original photography is not available at the start, use properly licensed temporary imagery during design. Replace it with approved OCSI assets before launch wherever practical.

## 7. Homepage Art Direction

The homepage should introduce OCSI quickly, demonstrate credibility, and guide visitors toward company information, capabilities, network details, careers, or contact.

### Proposed section order

1. Header and primary navigation
2. Hero section
3. Company introduction
4. Verified key strengths
5. Capabilities overview
6. Distribution network overview
7. Approved partner brands and recognition
8. People and careers
9. Closing call to action
10. Footer

### Hero concept

**Eyebrow:** Distribution That Connects

**Headline concept:** Moving Brands Forward.

**Supporting copy concept:** A proposed statement about connecting products, businesses, and communities through distribution.

**Primary CTA:** Discover OCSI

**Secondary CTA:** Explore Our Network

These are placeholder copy concepts, not approved company messaging. Final copy must be grounded in OCSI's actual positioning and approved by the company.

### Hero visual

Use a wide, authentic image of OCSI's distribution operations, team, or facilities. Consider an image-led composition with text aligned to one side rather than a generic centered headline over a darkened stock photo.

### Capability section

Use two or three strong content cards with:
- One clear heading.
- A short explanatory paragraph.
- A relevant, consistent image.
- A descriptive link.

Avoid presenting every capability as an identical icon card if photography can better demonstrate the business.

### Network section

Use a simplified geographic overview or branch highlights. Do not publish a map or coverage claim until the branch data has been validated.

### Credibility section

Use only verified proof points:
- Years of operation.
- Approved brand partnerships.
- Awards and recognitions.
- Verified operational or coverage figures.

Do not invent statistics or imply endorsement by another company.

## 8. Component and UI Styling

### Buttons

- Primary: forest green background with accessible light text, if palette is approved.
- Secondary: transparent or light surface with a clear border.
- Use concise action labels.
- Provide hover, focus, disabled, and active states.
- Maintain sufficiently large touch targets on mobile.

### Cards

- Use consistent internal spacing and image ratios.
- Prefer subtle borders or restrained shadows.
- Avoid excessive rounded corners if they make the site feel like a consumer app.
- Keep card content concise.
- Use descriptive links rather than making every element appear clickable.

### Navigation

- Simple desktop navigation.
- Clear active and focus states.
- Accessible mobile menu.
- Prominent Contact Us action without overloading the header.
- Avoid complicated mega menus unless content volume requires them.

### Forms

- Visible labels rather than placeholder-only labels.
- Clear validation and error messages.
- Accessible keyboard focus.
- Privacy information near submission where appropriate.
- Confirmation state after successful submission.
- Spam prevention that does not unnecessarily burden visitors.

### Icons

- Use one consistent icon family.
- Keep icons secondary to meaningful text and photography.
- Avoid mixing outline and filled icon styles without a deliberate system.

### Motion

- Use subtle transitions only when they improve feedback or comprehension.
- Avoid scroll-jacking, excessive parallax, or animation that delays access to content.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 10. WordPress Design and Build Considerations

The design should be realistic to maintain in WordPress.

### Recommended approach

- Lightweight custom theme.
- Gutenberg blocks for standard page content.
- Reusable patterns for common sections.
- Structured fields for branch and vacancy data.
- Consistent global styles for colors, typography, spacing, and buttons.
- Responsive image sizes and optimized assets.
- Minimal plugin dependencies.
- Editor-friendly content fields and documentation.

### CMS design principle

Give staff control over the content they need to update without requiring them to make complex layout decisions.

Examples:
- HR can publish, edit, and close job vacancies.
- Authorized staff can update branch addresses and contact details.
- Marketing or management can update approved company copy and images.
- Developers maintain templates, global styles, and more complex functionality.

### Performance considerations

- Avoid autoplay background videos unless justified.
- Use appropriately sized responsive images.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images where suitable.
- Limit unnecessary JavaScript and animation libraries.
- Test real page templates on mobile.
- Do not sacrifice accessibility or performance for decorative effects.

---

## 11. Accessibility and Quality Standards

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA practices where feasible.

- Maintain sufficient color contrast.
- Ensure keyboard navigation and visible focus states.
- Use semantic heading hierarchy.
- Label all form fields.
- Provide useful alternative text for meaningful images.
- Avoid text embedded only in images.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Ensure controls are usable on touch screens.
- Test mobile navigation, forms, and vacancy application flows.

Accessibility and performance should be part of the design process, not a final-stage patch.

## 13. Design Risks and Dependencies

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Existing brand guidelines conflict with proposed colors | Rework and inconsistent identity | Obtain logo and brand assets before finalizing the palette |
| No original photography | Generic or less credible presentation | Plan a photo collection or use licensed placeholders temporarily |
| Unverified business claims | Reputational risk | Require management approval for copy, figures, awards, and partnerships |
| Too much content on the homepage | Poor hierarchy and excessive scrolling | Prioritize key messages and link to detailed pages |
| Overly elaborate animation | Performance and accessibility problems | Use motion selectively and test reduced-motion behavior |
| Complex design difficult to edit in WordPress | High maintenance burden | Use reusable blocks, templates, and structured fields |
| Unclear ownership of content | Outdated branches or vacancies | Assign content owners and define an update process |
